# Is Networking More Effective Than Applying Online

Yes, by a wide margin — networking and direct outreach convert at meaningfully higher rates than applying cold through job boards, and a very large share of roles are filled through connections before (or instead of) ever being publicly posted.

## The numbers

Direct outreach and networking-based approaches show success rates roughly in the 30–80% range, compared to roughly 4–10% for applying to postings cold through job boards. Employee referrals in particular convert far better than any other channel — referred candidates are dramatically more likely to be hired than candidates who apply without one, and referral-sourced hires have the highest applicant-to-hire conversion rate of any channel. Job seekers who actively network also tend to have meaningfully shorter average job searches than those relying mainly on online applications.

## The "hidden job market"

A large share of roles — commonly cited around 70–80% — are filled without ever being publicly advertised at all, through internal moves, direct approaches, or referrals from someone already inside the company. Applying only to postings you can see online means missing this entire portion of the market by definition.

## What "networking" actually means in practice

It's broader than asking people for jobs directly, and mostly isn't that at all:

- Reaching out to former colleagues, classmates, or managers just to reconnect and mention you're exploring options.
- Requesting brief informational conversations with people in roles or companies you're interested in — not asking for a job, just learning and being visible.
- Engaging genuinely with your industry online (commenting, sharing relevant work, participating in discussions) rather than only posting when job-hunting.
- Asking people you already know for introductions to specific people or companies, rather than a general "let me know if you hear of anything."
- Attending industry events, meetups, or alumni gatherings where relevant people are already in the room.

## A practical split

A reasonable allocation of job-search time is roughly 60% toward networking, direct outreach, and follow-ups with existing contacts, with the remainder going toward tailored applications to posted roles. Online applications aren't wasted effort — they're just one channel among several, and not the highest-converting one.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

Job tracking here isn't limited to postings found online — a job sourced through a referral or a direct conversation logs the same way as one found on a job board, with the posting URL simply left blank, and the Contact person and Notes fields are a natural place to record who made the introduction and what was discussed. Since networking-sourced leads convert so much better, keeping them in the same pipeline as everything else (rather than tracked separately, or not tracked at all) makes it easier to see which ones deserve a timely follow-up.
