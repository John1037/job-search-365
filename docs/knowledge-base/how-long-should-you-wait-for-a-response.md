# How Long Should You Wait for a Response

## After submitting an application

It's normal to hear nothing for one to two weeks, and often longer — many employers don't reply at all to applicants they're not moving forward with. A week or two of silence after applying isn't a signal anything went wrong; it's the default.

## After an interview

Give it at least five business days before following up, and treat five to ten business days as the normal window before a check-in is reasonable. If the interviewer gave you a specific timeframe ("we'll be in touch next week," "expect to hear from us in two weeks"), give them the full window they named before following up — showing up early reads as impatience rather than interest.

General rule of thumb for the full process: expect roughly one to two weeks after an interview for a first response, though this varies enormously by company size, how many rounds are left, and internal scheduling.

## If you've already followed up once

If your first follow-up goes unanswered, wait another three to four days before a second, brief check-in. If you've sent a couple of follow-ups over two to three weeks with no reply at all, it's reasonable to treat the opportunity as not progressing and redirect your energy elsewhere — while still leaving the door open if they do eventually get back to you.

## A practical timeline

1. **Within 24 hours of an interview** — send a short, specific thank-you note referencing something from the actual conversation.
2. **5–10 business days after an interview with no update** — one polite follow-up.
3. **3–4 days after that, if still nothing** — at most one more brief check-in.
4. **2–3 weeks of total silence after following up** — move on mentally; keep the application live, but stop waiting on it.

Silence is genuinely common and rarely means an active rejection — hiring processes routinely stall for reasons that have nothing to do with the candidate (budget approvals, internal reorganization, a role being paused). It's still worth treating a long silence as a cue to keep applying elsewhere rather than pausing the rest of a search to wait for one answer.
