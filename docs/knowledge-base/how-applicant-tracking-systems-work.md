# How Applicant Tracking Systems Work

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software employers use to collect, sort, and rank applications before a person reviews them. It's extremely widely used — the large majority of large companies use one, and most mid-size companies do too — so understanding roughly how it works matters for almost any online application.

## What actually happens to an application

When a CV is uploaded, the ATS parses it into structured fields — name, contact details, job titles, employers, dates, skills, education — and compares that structured data against the job posting. Depending on the system, it may rank or score candidates by how closely their parsed data matches the role's requirements, before a recruiter ever opens the file. Modern systems increasingly go beyond simple keyword-counting to semantic and contextual matching, but keyword and phrase alignment with the posting still matters a great deal.

## What actually helps

- **Use the exact job title from the posting somewhere prominent** — it's one of the first things both the system and a human recruiter look for, and including it is linked to meaningfully higher callback rates.
- **Mirror the language of the posting** for skills, tools, and qualifications — using the same terms the employer used, not just a synonym, since parsing is more reliable on exact or near-exact matches.
- **Keep formatting simple and standard**: a single-column layout, conventional section headings (Experience, Education, Skills), and a text-based (not image-based) PDF or Word file. Complex multi-column layouts, text boxes, tables, and headers/footers can confuse parsing and cause real content to be dropped entirely.
- **Submit the file type the posting or portal expects** — usually PDF or `.docx`; an unusual format can fail to parse at all.

## What doesn't help

Simply repeating keywords unnaturally or "stuffing" them in white text or a hidden section doesn't work reliably on modern systems and can look worse to the human reviewer who eventually sees the file. The goal is genuine overlap between real experience and the posting's actual language, not gaming the count.

## A practical workflow

1. Keep one thorough master CV with everything.
2. For each application, pull the 15–20 most important keywords/phrases from that specific posting.
3. Check which already appear in the master CV in some form, and adjust wording so the ones that genuinely apply are stated in the posting's own terms.
4. Keep the visual format plain enough that a parser (not just a human) can read it correctly.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

Generating a CV for a specific job draws real skills, achievements, and phrasing from your own stored experience based on that job's actual description, which naturally produces the kind of genuine keyword overlap this document describes rather than artificial stuffing. On format: of the available templates, the single-column ones (Classic, Compact) are the safer choice for a role likely to be screened by an ATS, since the sidebar and multi-column layouts — while they look strong to a human reader — are exactly the kind of layout that can parse less reliably in older or stricter systems, per the formatting advice above.
