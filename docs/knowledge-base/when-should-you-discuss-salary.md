# When Should You Discuss Salary

The general rule: avoid leading with salary early on, but don't dodge it either if asked directly — and save serious negotiation for after a written offer, when you have the most leverage.

## Early interview stages

In a first interview, the focus should stay on fit and what you bring to the role, not compensation. If asked directly for a number early on, it's reasonable to give a researched range rather than a single figure, or to turn the question back to the employer by asking about the budgeted range for the role — many postings and application systems now disclose this upfront in places where pay-transparency rules apply.

By a second interview, it's generally fine to raise salary yourself if it hasn't come up, especially to confirm the role is in a workable range before investing more time — there's little point progressing through several rounds only to find a fundamental mismatch at the end.

## Why waiting for an offer matters

Leverage is highest once an employer has decided they want you specifically — that's the point where they have the most to lose by not agreeing on terms. Negotiating too early, before that decision is made, risks being screened out on price before you've had the chance to make the case for your value.

## When an offer arrives

- Don't accept on the spot, even if it sounds good. Thank them and ask for a reasonable window to consider it — 24–48 hours is standard and rarely questioned.
- Come prepared with researched market figures for the role, location, and experience level, and a clear minimum you'd actually accept, not just an aspirational number.
- Negotiate the whole package, not just base salary — signing bonus, start date, remote/hybrid terms, and review timeline are all legitimately negotiable too.

## If working with a recruiter or agency

It's normal, and often useful, to discuss salary expectations with a recruiter earlier than you would directly with an employer — they typically have real visibility into the role's actual budget and the company's flexibility, and can help calibrate expectations before an application even goes in.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

A job's detail page has fields for salary min/max, currency, type (annual/monthly/weekly/hourly), and basis (flat, estimated, or OTE), so a posted range — or a figure raised during the process — gets recorded against that specific job rather than relying on memory across a long, multi-stage search. Logging an "Offer received" event, with the details in Notes, keeps a record of exactly what was offered and when, which is useful if negotiation happens over more than one conversation or if you're weighing multiple offers logged across different jobs at once.
