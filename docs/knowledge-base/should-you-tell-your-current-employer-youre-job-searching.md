# Should You Tell Your Current Employer You're Job Searching

In almost all cases, no — not until you have a signed offer in hand, and often not even then until you're ready to resign. Keeping a search confidential while employed is standard, widely practiced, and nothing to feel guilty about.

## Why it's usually best kept quiet

Once an employer knows someone wants to leave, that person's position can change in ways that are hard to undo — trust can erode, they may quietly start succession or replacement planning, and in some cases the timeline to an exit gets compressed on the employer's terms rather than the employee's. None of that helps a search in progress, and most of it is avoidable simply by not disclosing until there's a firm reason to.

## Practical ways to keep a search confidential

- **Don't tell coworkers**, even ones you trust — information passed on informally is one of the most common ways a search becomes known to a manager who wasn't meant to find out.
- **Interview outside working hours** where possible — early mornings, evenings, lunch breaks — rather than taking unexplained time off that invites questions.
- **Be careful with anything that visibly changes your routine** — dressing differently on a day with no client meetings, disappearing for long unexplained blocks, and similar patterns are commonly what tips people off, more than any single interview itself.
- **Don't use work devices, work email, or work time** for search-related activity — a resume left on a shared printer or an overheard call are genuinely common ways searches get discovered.
- **On your CV**, consider listing your current employer more generically (industry/function rather than name) if it's shared broadly rather than sent directly and privately — this matters more for widely-distributed resumes than for a CV sent directly to one specific recruiter.
- **Be deliberate with LinkedIn** — updates to a profile, connecting with recruiters, or an "open to work" signal can all be visible depending on privacy settings; check what's public before making changes.
- **If working with a recruiter**, explicitly ask them to keep the search confidential — this is a completely normal request and one recruiters are used to handling.

## When telling your employer makes sense

Some situations are genuinely different — a manager who's already supportive of internal mobility, a company actively downsizing where being open is low-risk, or once an offer is signed and it's simply time to give notice. Outside of those specific situations, defaulting to confidentiality is the lower-risk approach.
