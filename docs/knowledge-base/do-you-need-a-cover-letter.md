# Do You Need a Cover Letter

It depends on who's reading it, and it's genuinely mixed: recruiters doing initial high-volume screening often skip cover letters entirely, while hiring managers and small-company founders — the people who actually own the decision — are considerably more likely to read them. Treat a cover letter as a tiebreaker and a chance to add context a CV can't, not as a formality every application needs.

## When a cover letter matters most

- **Roles where writing itself is part of the job** — marketing, communications, content, PR, policy — a strong cover letter doubles as a work sample.
- **Small companies and startups**, where a founder or hiring manager reads applications personally and a letter that shows real understanding of their specific situation stands out disproportionately.
- **Anything unusual about your application** — a career change, a gap, relocating, or applying somewhat outside your immediate experience — where a few sentences of context can pre-empt an objection a CV alone can't address.
- **When the posting explicitly asks for one.** Skipping a requested cover letter reads as not following instructions, regardless of how good the CV is.

## When it matters less

For high-volume roles at larger companies, especially anywhere applications are screened by recruiters or software before a hiring manager ever sees them, the CV and any screening-question answers typically carry far more weight than the letter.

## If you do write one, make it count

A personalized cover letter meaningfully increases callbacks; a generic, copy-pasted one barely moves the needle at all, and one that's obviously templated or error-ridden can actively count against you. Keep it short — roughly 150–250 words across three tight paragraphs is plenty:

1. Why this specific role, at this specific company — something that couldn't be pasted into any other application.
2. One concrete proof point connecting your actual experience to what the role needs.
3. A clean, brief close.

Most cover letters get skimmed for ten seconds or less, so the specific, non-generic sentence needs to be near the top, not buried at the end.
