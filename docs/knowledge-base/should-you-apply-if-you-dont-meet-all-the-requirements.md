# Should You Apply If You Don't Meet All the Requirements

In most cases, yes. Job postings routinely list a "wish list" of qualifications rather than a strict checklist, and employers often still consider candidates who don't tick every box, especially where transferable experience, projects, or strong soft skills can cover the gap.

## A rough guide by match percentage

- **75%+ of the listed qualifications** — apply. This is a strong candidacy; make sure the application clearly connects the experience you do have to what the role needs.
- **60–70%** — still worth applying, particularly if the missing pieces are things you could pick up quickly, or if you bring something the posting doesn't explicitly ask for but that's clearly valuable (relevant soft skills, adjacent experience, a strong track record of learning fast).
- **Well below that, especially on hard requirements** — be more selective. A missing requirement matters far more when it's a genuine gatekeeping criterion (a required license, a specific number of years of directly relevant experience, a degree the role legally requires) than when it's a "nice to have."

## The important exception

Not all requirements are equally soft. Some postings include hard knockout criteria — a required certification, a security clearance, a minimum years-of-experience threshold tied to legal or regulatory requirements — and no amount of a strong pitch overcomes those. Read the posting carefully enough to tell the difference between "preferred" and "required," and between a genuine hard requirement and one that's more aspirational.

## How to make the case when you're missing something

- Lead with strengths, not the gap — don't open by explaining what you lack.
- Draw a direct, explicit line between experience you do have and what the missing requirement is actually trying to achieve (e.g. a different tool used for the same underlying skill).
- Where relevant, a cover letter is a genuinely useful place to address this directly — a short paragraph connecting your real experience to the role's actual needs can outweigh a resume that looks like a partial match on paper.
- Don't fabricate experience or claim familiarity you don't have — it surfaces quickly in an interview and does far more damage than the original gap would have.

## Why this is worth doing anyway

You can't get a job you don't apply for, and hiring managers routinely hire candidates who didn't meet every listed criterion, especially when the candidate demonstrates the underlying capability clearly. Being overly conservative about which roles are "worth" applying to is one of the more common ways strong candidates filter themselves out unnecessarily.
