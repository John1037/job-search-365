# Should You Use AI to Write Your Applications

AI tools are now extremely common in job searching — a large majority of job seekers use them in some form — so the practical question isn't really "should you," it's "how much, and how visibly." Used to draft, structure, and polish, AI is broadly accepted. Used to generate a final, unedited, generic application, it's increasingly a liability.

## Why fully AI-generated applications backfire

Recruiters now read enormous numbers of AI-assisted cover letters and have developed a strong eye for the tells: a generic opening, "both-sides" balanced phrasing, enthusiasm that doesn't reference anything specific about the role or company, and a certain flat uniformity of sentence structure. Beyond human pattern recognition, some employers also run submitted text through AI-detection tools. A meaningful share of employers say they'll reject an application that reads as templated or obviously unpersonalized — and because this often happens as a quiet auto-rejection, the candidate never finds out why.

## What works

- Use AI to get past a blank page, restructure a draft, tighten wording, or check grammar and tone.
- Feed it your own real achievements, specifics, and voice — don't let it invent generic claims or a generic narrative you then submit unedited.
- Personalize every AI-assisted draft with something that couldn't apply to any other application: a specific detail about the role, the team, or the company.
- Read the final version aloud before sending — if it sounds like it could've been sent to anyone, it needs another pass.

## What to avoid

- Submitting an AI draft unedited, especially a cover letter — this is exactly the pattern experienced recruiters and detection tools are tuned to catch.
- Letting AI fabricate or embellish experience, skills, or achievements you don't actually have — it fails immediately once discussed in an interview.
- Using the same AI-generated block of text across many applications with only the company name swapped — this is the single most detectable pattern.

## The honest framing

The candidates who benefit most from AI tools in 2026 use them for efficiency and structure while supplying the actual substance themselves — real accomplishments, specific reasons for applying, and their own voice. That combination reads as a well-prepared, thoughtful application. A wholly AI-written one, however fluent, increasingly reads as exactly that.
