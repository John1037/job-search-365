# Should You Tailor Your CV for Every Application

Yes — but "tailoring" means targeted edits, not writing a new CV from scratch each time. Start from one thorough master CV containing everything, then adjust what's shown and how it's worded for each specific job.

## Why it matters

Most mid-size and larger employers screen applications with software before a human ever sees them, and those systems rank candidates partly by how closely a CV's language matches the job posting's. A generic CV can genuinely miss the exact terms a screener is looking for even when the underlying experience is a good fit. On the human side, a recruiter typically spends well under a minute on an initial scan, so the most relevant material needs to be immediately visible, not buried under things that don't matter for this particular role.

## What to actually change

Tailoring is a handful of surgical edits, not a rewrite:

1. Pull 15–20 priority keywords and phrases straight from the job description (skills, tools, certifications, the exact job title).
2. Work the ones that genuinely apply into your summary, skills list, and relevant bullet points — in your own words, describing real experience, not just stuffed in.
3. Reorder or re-prioritize which skills, achievements, and past roles appear most prominently, based on relevance to this specific job.
4. For older or less relevant roles, compress them rather than deleting them — a shorter summary line still shows continuity without crowding out what's actually relevant now.

## What not to do

- Don't fabricate experience or skills to match keywords — it fails as soon as it's tested in an interview.
- Don't reorganize the entire structure/format for every single application — that's where the time actually goes; keep the shape consistent and change the content.
- Don't apply to the exact same posting with the same CV twice hoping a resend helps — it doesn't change the score.

## The time-vs-return trade-off

Tailoring every field on every application doesn't scale past a handful of applications a day, and it doesn't need to — the biggest gains come from getting the job title, top skills, and summary aligned, which takes a few minutes once a master CV exists to draw from. That's a far better use of limited time than applying to more roles with an untailored CV.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

This is exactly what the CV-building feature on a job's detail page automates. Build a master library once — profile summary, skills, experience, education, certifications, custom sections — then generate a CV for any specific job and the AI selects and arranges only what's relevant to that job's description, drawing solely from real, already-stored content rather than inventing anything. Older roles can be shown in full detail or compacted to a one-line summary, matching the "keep the shape, change the content" approach above. Everything's editable in a review step before rendering, and the result can be regenerated for a different job in a couple of minutes rather than a rewrite from scratch.
