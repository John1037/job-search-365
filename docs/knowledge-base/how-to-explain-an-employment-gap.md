# How to Explain an Employment Gap

A gap in your work history is common and, on its own, rarely disqualifies a candidate — employers generally focus far more on skills, experience, and how clearly a career path is explained than on the mere existence of a gap. The goal is to address it briefly and honestly, then move the conversation forward.

## On the CV

- Don't try to hide a gap of more than a few months with vague or misleading dates — it tends to look worse when it's noticed (and it usually is) than a gap that's simply accounted for.
- For a longer gap (roughly a year or more), consider giving it its own short line in the experience section, the same way a job would be listed — a couple of words on what the time was spent on (caregiving, further study, travel, freelance work, a layoff and search) is usually enough.
- Keep the explanation brief — one or two lines, not a paragraph. The CV's job is to get you an interview, not to resolve every question up front.
- If you used the time productively — courses, certifications, freelance or volunteer work, a personal project — include it. It shows the gap wasn't idle and can double as relevant material in its own right.

## In interviews

- Be honest; a background or reference check will typically surface the real timeline, and an inconsistency discovered later damages trust far more than the gap itself would have.
- State the reason plainly and briefly, then pivot to what you did with the time or what you learned, and back to why you're a strong fit now. Dwelling on it or over-explaining tends to draw more attention to it, not less.
- Keep the explanation consistent across your CV, cover letter, and what you say in conversation — small inconsistencies are more noticeable than the gap itself.

## Common, entirely normal reasons

Layoffs, caregiving responsibilities, health reasons, further education, relocation, or a deliberate career break are all common and are not, by themselves, red flags to most reasonable employers. The way it's handled — direct, brief, and forward-looking — matters far more than the underlying reason.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

A custom CV section is a natural place to account for a gap in one or two lines — a short "Career Break" or similarly-titled section works the same way a job entry would, exactly as recommended above, and it's part of the same reusable library every generated CV can draw from rather than something to retype per application. If the gap involved something worth showing in detail — a course, a certification, freelance or volunteer work — that belongs in its own entry (education, certifications, or another custom section) so it can be surfaced as real, relevant content rather than just an explanation.
