# Should You Send a Thank-You Note After an Interview

Yes. It's not strictly required, but it makes a measurable difference, very few candidates actually do it, and skipping it has genuinely cost people offers.

## Why it matters more than it might seem

A large majority of hiring managers say a thank-you note positively influences their decision, yet only a small minority of candidates actually send one — meaning it's a low-effort way to stand out simply by doing something most applicants skip. On the other side, a meaningful share of hiring managers have ruled out a candidate specifically for not sending one, particularly at the more junior end where follow-through is treated as a signal in itself.

## Timing

Send it within 24 hours of the interview, while the conversation is still fresh for both sides and while you're still front-of-mind among whichever other candidates were interviewed around the same time.

## What makes one actually effective

The bar isn't "send a thank-you" — it's "send a specific one." A generic template ("Thank you for your time, I look forward to hearing from you") reads as an afterthought. A strong one:

- References something specific from the actual conversation — a topic discussed, a challenge the team mentioned, something you learned that reinforced your interest.
- Briefly reinforces why you're a strong fit, ideally tying back to something raised in the interview rather than repeating the CV.
- Stays short — a few sentences is enough; it's a courtesy and a reminder, not another writing sample.
- Goes to everyone you spoke with, personalized per person where you had substantively different conversations, rather than one identical note copied to multiple people.

## Format

Email is the standard, expected format for most roles — it's fast, easy for the interviewer to act on or forward internally, and doesn't require guessing at someone's mailing address. A handwritten note can be a nice extra touch in some contexts but shouldn't replace a prompt email, since timing matters more than the medium.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

Logging an "Interview completed" event captures the type of interview and its date straight away, and the job's Notes field is a good place to jot down what was actually discussed while it's fresh — exactly the specific detail a strong thank-you note needs, rather than trying to recall it a day later. The profile's dedicated application email address is also what a reply would land in, kept separate from a personal or work inbox that isn't meant for job-search correspondence.
