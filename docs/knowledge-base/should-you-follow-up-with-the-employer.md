# Should You Follow Up With the Employer

Yes — following up is expected, generally viewed positively, and costs almost nothing to do well. The key is timing and tone: a follow-up should read as continued genuine interest, not pressure.

## When to follow up

- **After applying, with no acknowledgment:** it's usually fine to wait one to two weeks before a brief check-in, since many companies simply don't confirm receipt.
- **After an interview:** wait at least five business days (five to ten is the safer range) unless the interviewer gave you a specific date to expect news by — then wait for that date first.
- **After a recruiter or hiring manager said they'd follow up "soon" and didn't:** a short, polite nudge a few days after their stated timeframe is reasonable.

## How to do it well

- Keep it short — a few sentences, not a re-pitch of your whole candidacy.
- Reference something specific from the application or interview (the role title, a topic discussed) rather than a generic "just checking in."
- Restate interest briefly and ask a direct, low-pressure question: whether there's an update, or whether a decision timeline has shifted.
- Use email as the default channel unless you were told to use something else — it's easy for the recipient to act on, forward, or file.

## What to avoid

- Don't follow up multiple times in quick succession — space attempts out (three to four days apart at minimum after an initial follow-up), and stop after two or three attempts with no reply at all.
- Don't follow up by phone unless that's the relationship you already have with that contact — an unexpected call can read as more intrusive than an email.
- Don't frame the follow-up as a complaint about the wait, even if the silence is genuinely long — keep it entirely forward-looking.

## Why it's worth doing

A follow-up is one of the few low-cost, low-risk actions in a job search that can genuinely move things forward — it can surface a stalled process, put a candidate back in front of a busy hiring manager, or simply confirm there's nothing more to wait for so effort can go elsewhere. Employers broadly expect candidates to follow up and rarely penalize a well-timed, well-worded one.

## How JobSearch 365 helps

A job's detail page has a dedicated Contact person field for exactly this — who to actually follow up with — plus a free-text Notes field for jotting down what's already been said, so a follow-up references the real conversation instead of a vague "just checking in." Dashboard alerts, driven by whatever's logged for that job, are also a natural trigger to actually send the follow-up rather than letting it slip while the search moves on to other applications.
